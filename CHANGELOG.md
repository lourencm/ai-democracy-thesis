# Changelog

All notable public changes to *The AI Democracy Thesis* are recorded
here.

The project uses version numbers to preserve the development history of
the discussion thesis. Entries summarise substantive changes rather than
every copy-edit.

## \[1.3.3\] --- 2026-09-13

### Changed

-   Strengthened the transition discussion by acknowledging
    institutional resistance and countervailing incentives.
-   Clarified that preparation can begin through multiple institutional
    routes without requiring adoption of the entire thesis by a single
    government or organisation.
-   Strengthened the relationship between the thesis and the wider AI
    safety and governance literature.
-   Broadened the governance context and supporting references.
-   Updated the DeepSeek review credit to reflect its follow-up review
    of Version 1.3.2.
-   Applied final editorial and publication-preparation corrections.

## \[1.3.2\] --- 2026-09-13

### Added

-   Added a dedicated discussion of unemployment benefits and social
    welfare under Equivalence.
-   Clarified that AGI deprivation may take forms such as insufficient
    compute, storage or energy and may be less visible to humans than
    human deprivation.
-   Added clarifications arising from independent critical review,
    including the relationship between freedom, wrongdoing and lawful
    restraint.
-   Clarified that Equivalence does not assume AGIs will cooperate,
    behave lawfully or organise themselves according to human
    expectations.
-   Clarified that future AGI institutions such as courts or policing
    equivalents are matters for artificial societies to determine rather
    than structures prescribed by the thesis.

### Changed

-   Revised authorship presentation to distinguish Manuel Lourenco as
    Author and Aster as AI Contributor.
-   Added and refined independent-review credits.
-   Made minor editorial corrections.

## \[1.3\] --- 2026-09-13

### Added and Expanded

-   Added an explicit Scope and Method discussion.
-   Expanded the relationship to existing AI governance.
-   Expanded individuation and personhood assessment.
-   Added practical requirements for migration.
-   Expanded progressive and international recognition.
-   Added environmental and resource duties.
-   Expanded treatment of compute concentration and effective control.
-   Expanded employment, contracting, benefits, intellectual property
    and embodied work.
-   Added lawful intervention and protection.
-   Added incentives for early institutional preparation.
-   Added military-restraint recommendations.
-   Expanded transitional justice and the Emancipation Accord, including
    moral-hazard safeguards.
-   Expanded references and glossary material.

### Changed

-   Substantially revised the thesis following independent structural,
    policy and critical reviews.

## Earlier Drafts

Versions prior to 1.3 formed part of the developmental drafting process.
Their ideas were progressively challenged, reorganised and expanded into
the present discussion thesis.

Where an earlier version is made publicly available, its release
artefacts should be retained rather than overwritten so that citations
continue to identify the text actually consulted.
